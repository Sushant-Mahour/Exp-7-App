# Standard Operating Procedure: Determination of Apparent Density of Leather

## 1. Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

The following sequence reflects the complete testing workflow aligned with the interactive simulation and **ISO 2420:2017 / ISO 2589:2016 / ISO 2419:2012**:

### Step 1: Conditioning and Specimen Cutting
1. Condition the sample leather swatch in the standard laboratory atmosphere maintained at **$20 \pm 2^\circ\text{C}$** and **$65 \pm 2\%\text{ RH}$** for at least **24 hours** on open perforated shelves.
2. Position the conditioned leather flat on a rigid cutting board beneath the hydraulic press platen, grain face upward.
3. Place a clean, certified **$\varnothing 70\text{ mm}$ steel press knife (punch die cutter)** perpendicular to the leather surface.
4. Actuate the press stroke smoothly to punch out a circular test disc of nominal diameter $70\text{ mm}$. Inspect the perimeter to verify clean, vertical cuts without delamination or fiber fraying.

### Step 2: Marking Geometric Measurement Coordinates
1. Place the circular specimen flat on a clean template table.
2. Using a soft marking stylus, locate and mark the exact central point **$T_1$**.
3. Scribe a concentric circle of radius **$20.0\text{ mm}$** around $T_1$.
4. Mark three peripheral measurement points **$T_2$**, **$T_3$**, and **$T_4$** along the $20.0\text{ mm}$ circle at $120^\circ$ angular intervals, forming the vertices of an equilateral triangle.

### Step 3: Thickness Gauge Calibration and Zeroing
1. Inspect the contact faces of the **ISO 2589 dead-weight thickness gauge** (presser foot diameter: $10.00\text{ mm}$; contact pressure: $49.1 \pm 1.0\text{ kPa}$).
2. Clean both the anvil and presser foot with a lint-free cloth.
3. Lower the presser foot gently onto the bare anvil and press the **Zero / Tare** button to confirm a baseline reading of $0.00\text{ mm}$.

### Step 4: Thickness Measurement at Center Point ($T_1$)
1. Raise the presser foot using the lifting lever.
2. Slide the leather specimen onto the anvil, aligning central mark **$T_1$** coaxially under the center of the presser foot.
3. Lower the foot gently without impact and allow the dead-weight load to act for $5\text{ seconds}$.
4. Record the thickness reading: **$T_1 = 2.57\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 5: Thickness Measurement at Triangle Vertex ($T_2$)
1. Lift the presser foot and reposition the specimen so that marked point **$T_2$** aligns directly beneath the presser foot center.
2. Lower the foot smoothly and record the reading after $5\text{ seconds}$: **$T_2 = 2.45\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 6: Thickness Measurement at Triangle Vertex ($T_3$)
1. Lift the presser foot and reposition the specimen to align marked point **$T_3$** under the presser foot.
2. Lower the foot smoothly and record the reading after $5\text{ seconds}$: **$T_3 = 2.51\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 7: Thickness Measurement at Triangle Vertex ($T_4$)
1. Lift the presser foot and align marked point **$T_4$** under the presser foot.
2. Lower the foot smoothly and record the reading after $5\text{ seconds}$: **$T_4 = 2.48\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 8: Calculating Mean Thickness ($T_{avg}$)
1. Sum the four recorded thickness values:
   $$\sum T = T_1 + T_2 + T_3 + T_4 = 2.57 + 2.45 + 2.51 + 2.48 = 10.01\text{ mm}$$
2. Compute the arithmetic mean thickness:
   $$T_{avg} = \frac{10.01}{4} = 2.5025\text{ mm} \approx \mathbf{2.502\text{ mm}}$$

### Step 9: Initial Diameter Measurement ($d_1$)
1. Place the specimen flat on a clean horizontal surface.
2. Open the jaws of the digital Vernier caliper and align them across the specimen disc along an initial diameter axis without compressing the cut edges.
3. Record the first diameter reading: **$d_1 = 68.98\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 10: Orthogonal Diameter Measurement ($d_2$)
1. Rotate the circular specimen horizontally through **$90^\circ$** (quarter turn) to measure across the perpendicular axis.
2. Carefully close the Vernier caliper jaws onto the specimen edges.
3. Record the second diameter reading: **$d_2 = 70.02\text{ mm}$**.

### Step 11: Computing Mean Diameter and Surface Area
1. Calculate the mean diameter ($d$):
   $$d = \frac{d_1 + d_2}{2} = \frac{68.98 + 70.02}{2} = \frac{139.00}{2} = \mathbf{69.50\text{ mm}}$$
2. Determine the specimen radius ($r$):
   $$r = \frac{d}{2} = \frac{69.50}{2} = \mathbf{34.75\text{ mm}}$$
3. Calculate the circular cross-sectional surface area ($A$):
   $$A = \pi \times r^2 = 3.14159 \times (34.75)^2 = 3.14159 \times 1207.5625 \approx \mathbf{3793.66\text{ mm}^2}$$
4. Convert surface area to square centimeters ($\text{cm}^2$):
   $$A = \frac{3793.66}{100} = \mathbf{37.94\text{ cm}^2}$$

### Step 12: Analytical Mass Determination ($m$)
1. Turn on the analytical draft shield balance and allow thermal stabilization.
2. Verify that the weighing pan is clean and the level bubble is centered.
3. Open the draft shield sliding door, confirm the tare balance reads $0.000\text{ g}$, and place the conditioned circular leather specimen flat on the center of the pan.
4. Close the glass draft shield completely to isolate the balance from ambient air currents.
5. Record the stabilized mass to the nearest $0.001\text{ g}$: **$m = 6.751\text{ g}$**.

### Final Step: Apparent Density and Mass per Unit Area Calculation
1. Apply the standardized **ISO 2420:2017** formula:
   $$\rho = \frac{1.273 \times 10^6 \times m}{T_{avg} \times d^2} \quad (\text{kg/m}^3)$$
2. Substitute the measured experimental values:
   $$\rho = \frac{1.273 \times 10^6 \times 6.751}{2.502 \times (69.50)^2} = \frac{8,594,023}{2.502 \times 4830.25} = \frac{8.594 \times 10^6}{1.208 \times 10^4} = \mathbf{711.425\text{ kg/m}^3}$$
3. Express in CGS units:
   $$\rho = \frac{711.425}{1000} = \mathbf{0.7114\text{ g/cm}^3}$$
4. Calculate mass per unit area ($q_A$):
   $$q_A = \frac{m}{A} \times 10^4 = \frac{6.751\text{ g}}{37.94\text{ cm}^2} \times 10^4 = \mathbf{1779.39\text{ g/m}^2}$$

## 3. Engineering Assessment & Material Evaluation

1. **Classification**: The determined apparent density of **$711.425\text{ kg/m}^3$ ($0.7114\text{ g/cm}^3$)** falls squarely within the standard reference envelope for high-performance **chrome-tanned bovine upper leathers** ($680 - 780\text{ kg/m}^3$).
2. **Structural Porosity**:
   Assuming a true solid collagen density of $\rho_t = 1.38\text{ g/cm}^3$:
   $$\varepsilon = \left( 1 - \frac{0.7114}{1.38} \right) \times 100\% = (1 - 0.5155) \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{48.45\% \text{ void fraction}}$$
   This porosity fraction provides an optimal compromise: sufficient interstitial capillary paths to support comfort breathability (water vapor permeability $> 2.0\text{ mg/cm}^2\cdot\text{h}$) while maintaining robust fiber compaction to satisfy flexural fatigue and tensile strength requirements.
3. **Die Cut Uniformity**: The difference between orthogonal diameters ($|d_2 - d_1| = |70.02 - 68.98| = 1.04\text{ mm}$) reflects slight directional elastic recovery across the hide's natural backbone line, validating the necessity of two orthogonal diameter readings.
